Ricky Watts Mural

Artist
Ricky Watts
Location
Building 5 — Fairfield Ave Entrance
Completed
2021 — SHINE Mural Festival

This massive mural was painted by artist Ricky Watts during the 2021 SHINE Mural Festival.

The wall might look straightforward, but when Ricky arrived it was actually full of obstacles — electrical boxes, lighting, security cameras, landscaping, and even a door frame sticking two feet out from the wall. Despite all of that, he completed the mural in just five days using around 170 cans of spray paint.

The glowing oranges and yellows in the background are meant to reflect Florida’s famous sunrises and sunsets — something the artist says always amazes him when he visits the state.

And the artwork doesn’t stop at the wall. The mural actually continues onto the ground — about 2,300 square feet of painted pavement, completed in just two days with help from local mural legends, the Vitale Brothers.

Look For

Did you notice the mural continues onto the ground? Murals like this are designed to change depending on where you’re standing, so artists often think about the entire environment — not just the wall.

Cosmic Beams & Sunshine Dreams

Artist
Ashley Cantero
Location
FloridaRAMA Building 5 Lobby
Completed
2024

This mural is by Ashley Cantero, a Tampa-based artist known for her incredibly precise line work and bold characters.

You’ll notice the central figure here — a strong female character surrounded by radiant shapes and beams of light. Ashley often focuses on feminine power and emotional storytelling in her work.

Her style pulls inspiration from a mix of places: 1960s psychedelic art, Atomic Age design, and classic science fiction imagery. So you get this really fun blend of retro nostalgia and futuristic imagination.

The title, “Cosmic Beams and Sunshine Dreams,” really captures that feeling — part outer space, part Florida sunshine.

This is the largest mural here on the property, taking up 8 walls! It was created in collaboration with FloridaRAMA and contains lots of hidden references to what you’ll see and experience inside of it.

Look For

If you’ve already been inside FloridaRAMA, see how many elements you can spot that tie into the experience — space, cats, retro futurism, eyeball flowers, the Jellyfish girl.

What decade inspired this mural?
Most people guess the 60s because of the psychedelic influence — the swirls, bold colors, and cosmic themes. It’s really more of a 50s – 60s retro futuristic style and vibe.

Frankie G — “Hearts Were Meant to Fly”

Artist
Frankie G
Location
Building 6 Boardwalk
Completed
2024 — SHINE 10

This mural was created by Frankie G, a Miami-born artist who now lives and works in Tampa.

Frankie’s work often blends playfulness, nostalgia, and personal storytelling, and this mural is actually a tribute to his Cuban heritage and to a family member he recently lost.

You’ll see a lot of dreamlike elements: floating shapes, bright colors, and symbols like the paper airplane, which represents memories, journeys, and the idea that our hearts and dreams travel beyond us.

During the SHINE Festival kickoff, a local artist actually dressed up as a living version of the mural, complete with a paper airplane headpiece.

Look For

Take a quick look around the mural — can you spot the paper airplane? Artists love hiding little symbols like that. Once you see them, you can’t unsee them.

Florida Wildlife Corridor Mural

Artist
Ernesto Maranje
Location
Building 7
Completed
2022

This mural was created by Ernesto Maranje, a Cuban-American artist known for painting wildlife and ecosystems.

The main character here is the great blue heron, painted larger than life. Ernesto chose it because the silhouette of a heron is so elegant and recognizable.

Surrounding it are animals native to Florida — including a black bear, river otter, gopher tortoise, alligator, and swallow-tailed kite.

The mural was created in partnership with the Florida Wildlife Corridor Foundation, an organization working to protect an enormous network of wilderness stretching across the entire state.

St. Pete: West to East

Artists
Vitale Brothers & Sionna
Location
Building 5 Trail Side
Completed
2025 — SHINE 11

This mural tells a visual story of St. Petersburg’s history.

It was created by the Vitale Brothers, longtime St. Pete muralists, along with artist Sionna.

The mural reads like a timeline moving from west to east, starting with the beaches and working toward downtown.

You’ll see references to places like the Thunderbird Hotel on Treasure Island, the iconic pink Don CeSar Hotel, historic orange groves, and even Webb City, which was once a famous tourist attraction in early St. Pete history.

Look For

Have you visited the Don CeSar Hotel before? If you have, you probably remember that iconic pink color — it’s one of the most recognizable buildings on Florida’s Gulf Coast.

Museum of Motherhood Mural

Artist
Raisa Nosova
Location
Building 7 — Fairfield Side
Completed
2025

This mural was created by Raisa Nosova, a New York-based international artist whose work often explores the realities and emotional complexities of motherhood.

She painted this after meeting Martha Joy Rose, the founder of the Museum of Motherhood, which is located here at The Factory.

Raisa’s work often addresses topics society doesn’t always talk about openly — things like the emotional challenges of parenting, maternal strength, and the deep bonds between parent and child.

The mural explores themes of protection, endurance, and the passage of time.

Reflect

Art can mean different things to different people — when you look at this mural, what emotion does it give you? That’s one of the things artists like Raisa love — when viewers bring their own experiences into the artwork.

Photo: The Path of the Wild

Dulk — “The Path of the Wild”

Artist
Dulk
Location
Building 2
Completed
2025

This incredible mural was created by internationally known Spanish artist Dulk.

It’s called “The Path of the Wild,” and it features many animals native to Florida — including manatees, a Florida panther, alligators, turtles, otters, ibis, and black bears.

Because the wall is so long, Dulk used it to create a sense of movement, almost like the animals are migrating together through the landscape.

The mural highlights the idea behind the Florida Wildlife Corridor, which helps protect the natural pathways animals need to survive.

Think About

Out of all the animals here, which one do you think represents Florida the most?

One answer that surprises people
Most people say the manatee or alligator, but the Florida panther is actually one of the most endangered.

FloridaRAMA Entrance Mural

Artist
Chad Mize
Location
FloridaRAMA Entrance — Building 6

Before you head inside, take a look at this mural by St. Petersburg artist Chad Mize. One of his most recognizable characters is Mr. Sun, who you can see all around the city, including on the Sunrunner buses that connect downtown St. Pete to the beaches.

Chad is known for his playful doodle style, which mixes cartoon characters, bold colors, and lots of hidden details. If you look closely at this mural, you’ll notice that it’s bursting with bright colors and strange, funny characters.

But this mural isn’t random — it actually acts like a preview of the adventure you’re about to go on inside FloridaRAMA.

You might notice creatures like merpeople, space cats, sweet treats and other strange characters. These are little hints at the weird and wonderful things you’ll encounter inside the experience.

Think of this mural like the cover of a book or the trailer for a movie. It gives you a glimpse of what’s coming, but it doesn’t reveal everything yet.

Spotting Challenge

• a space cat
• something that looks like it belongs in outer space

Artists like Chad often hide little surprises like this so people keep discovering things every time they visit.

FloridaRAMA Exit Doodle Wall

Artist
Chad Mize
Location
FloridaRAMA Exit / Gift Shop — Building 6

Now that you’ve explored FloridaRAMA, you’ve arrived at the second mural by Chad Mize, and this one is a little different.

Instead of bright colors, this mural is done entirely in black and white, and it’s filled with hundreds of little doodles.

While the entrance mural was a preview, this one is more like a recap of the entire experience.

You might start recognizing things from inside FloridaRAMA — strange creatures, characters, symbols, and scenes that remind you of places you visited.

It’s almost like a giant eye-spy puzzle where you can look around and spot things you remember.

Mural Eye-Spy

See if you can find something in the mural that reminds you of something you saw inside FloridaRAMA. When you find one, point to it!

You’ll probably notice that everyone finds something different — that’s part of the fun.

Wrapping Up the Tour

Today you saw murals made by artists from Florida, across the United States, and around the world. And every one of them told a different kind of story.

Some murals told stories about nature and wildlife. Some were about history and the city of St. Petersburg. Some were about family, memories, and personal experiences. And others were just meant to spark imagination and creativity.

One of the coolest things about murals is that they turn ordinary walls into giant outdoor art galleries that anyone can experience — no museum ticket required. And here’s the most important part: murals don’t just decorate a place — they tell the story of the community.

So the next time you see a mural somewhere, try asking yourself:

  • What story is the artist telling?
  • What details can I discover?
  • And how does it make me feel?

Because once you start looking at art that way… you’ll start seeing stories everywhere.
